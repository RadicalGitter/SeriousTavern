# Roleplay setting design

Read this reference when inventing or substantially revising a complete
setting, ensemble, route structure, opening, or campaign pressure system.

## Experience before lore

A roleplay setting is not a fact collection. It is a renewable arrangement of
pressures, relationships, permissions, and incomplete knowledge that gives the
player meaningful things to notice, ask, attempt, refuse, and care about.

Start with an intent anchor:

> Let the player repeatedly experience a valued activity or tension while
> retaining important agency, amid people and institutions that create
> independent pressure.

The anchor should still work if every proper noun changes. Lore earns context
space only when it helps portray the present, constrain a consequence, support
a choice, or make a relationship distinct.

Record a compact authority ledger before inventing connective material:

| Claim | Class | Source | May the model see it now? | Promotion owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| accepted setting fact | accepted | named authority | yes or gated | owner/project |
| current campaign fact | play | reviewed transcript/state | yes | chat state |
| connective invention | provisional | setting build | if approved | owner/project |
| generated candidate | proposal | model/tool | no by default | reviewer |
| reference texture | external | named source | bounded | never self-promotes |

Use the owning project's class names when they differ.

## The setting pocket and dramatic engine

For a broad original world or established franchise, choose a bounded pocket:
a city, ship, expedition, school, frontier, court, company, refuge, district,
or network of a few connected places. The larger world supplies horizon and
pressure without entering every prompt.

Write the dramatic engine as a relationship among forces rather than a planned
sequence:

> When a recurring pressure intensifies, actors with incompatible promises
> must decide a concrete tradeoff, while a named constraint prevents easy
> settlement.

A useful engine supplies all of these:

- a recurring pressure that can appear in different forms;
- multiple actors able to initiate action without the protagonist;
- scarce time, trust, access, safety, legitimacy, knowledge, or resources;
- consequences that change relationships and future options;
- enough stability for rest, humor, daily life, and recovery;
- no single secret reveal whose completion exhausts the setting.

Test the engine by sketching five different ordinary scenes. If each requires
the same villain attack or quest giver, the engine is an outline in disguise.

## Route topology

Routes may be ideological factions, careers, loyalties, methods, communities,
or personal commitments. They need not be announced as routes in play.

For every major route record:

| Dimension | Question |
| --- | --- |
| promise | What genuine good, need, or freedom can this route provide? |
| method | How does it normally pursue that promise? |
| cost | What does participation demand or endanger? |
| constituency | Who benefits, depends on it, or makes it worth saving? |
| harm | Who is overlooked, constrained, or exploited? |
| internal debate | Which members disagree without becoming traitors? |
| early invitation | What can the player try without permanent commitment? |
| disillusionment | What honest failure could make the player reconsider? |
| repair | How could the route change rather than merely be abandoned? |
| exit consequence | What obligations survive departure? |

Keep route signals separate from route locks. Visiting, training, working with,
or accepting help from a group is not permanent allegiance unless play makes
that choice explicit. An independent route must create practical obligations
and vulnerabilities; it is not a cost-free refusal to participate.

## Player authorship

Choose how much of the protagonist is authored before play:

- **Open:** role and immediate circumstance only.
- **Lightly authored:** a few competencies, ties, and pressures, with motives
  and identity left to the player.
- **Established:** a defined protagonist whose speech, thoughts, and choices
  still belong to the player.

Record what the narrator may infer from visible action and what it must never
assign. External honesty normally excludes unstated thoughts, feelings,
attraction, sincerity, realization, bodily reaction, dialogue, consent, and
irreversible action. Consequences may affect the protagonist; authorship of the
protagonist's response remains with the player.

Avoid chosen-one shortcuts unless explicitly requested. Power, status, or
special access should create social and practical pressure rather than settle
moral worth.

## Starting without a prologue

Use one of four opening modes:

1. **Continuity seed — default.** Author a compact accepted past and begin just
   after a consequential event, when relationships and obligations already
   have texture.
2. **Cold open.** Begin at the first disruption with little established history
   beyond ordinary life.
3. **Workshop prologue.** Play a bounded prelude to discover voice, themes, and
   player decisions, then review a factual harvest.
4. **Imported continuity.** Curate an existing transcript or campaign record
   into accepted state and unresolved threads.

A continuity seed should usually establish:

- three to eight consequential past facts;
- one recent event or decision that created present motion;
- one to three active relationships, each with a concrete choice or debt;
- current place, time, present cast, conditions, possessions, and evidence;
- two to five commitments, discoveries, or unresolved threads;
- one immediate activity or pressure that invites action without presenting a
  menu.

Do not imply that authored seed facts were literally played. Label their source
as authored setup. Keep identity-defining choices open unless the user approved
them. A seed is too large when the player must study a biography before acting.

A workshop prologue is useful when voice and moral direction are the unknowns,
not as a ritual every setting must perform. Harvest separately:

- facts established by the player;
- setting facts accepted during play;
- themes revealed by decisions;
- relationship signals;
- unresolved questions;
- state at the handoff;
- candidate examples and whether they are safe to reuse.

Everything else remains transcript color or a proposal.

## Institutions and factions

An institution becomes playable when it contains people, services, procedures,
blind spots, and internal disagreement—not when it receives a long history.

For each important institution define:

- public promise and material service;
- actual decision process and who can say no;
- the good it performs on an ordinary day;
- the pressure that bends or corrupts it;
- internal factions or professional disagreements;
- what it wants from the protagonist;
- what it can offer without ownership;
- what evidence would change a reasonable member's mind;
- how a person can leave, resist, reform, or be harmed by it.

Avoid monolithic moral alignment. An institution may be necessary, compromised,
reformable, plural, or predatory in different parts without becoming vague.

## Ensemble design

Design characters from story vectors rather than demographic slots or party
roles. Use this dossier:

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| practical role | Gives the person a reason to be present and useful. |
| visible competence | Lets introduction happen through action, not biography. |
| present desire | Creates motion now. |
| independent obligation | Keeps life from orbiting the protagonist. |
| moral belief | Produces defensible choices and disagreement. |
| contradiction or blind spot | Makes the belief costly rather than decorative. |
| hard boundary | Defines a credible refusal or departure. |
| quest pressure | Can progress elsewhere and return with consequences. |
| relational vectors | Names possible friendship, rivalry, trust, mentorship, attraction, or incompatibility without guaranteeing any. |
| introduction situation | Connects the person to a concrete problem already in motion. |
| causal fallback | Allows a later meeting without teleporting or forcing it. |
| reason to stay or leave | Makes continued travel a choice with logistics. |
| visibility layers | Separates public core, contextual facets, mutable state, clues, and prompt-absent truth. |

The quest pressure should expose character, not merely provide an errand. It
often works best as a collision between the person's belief and obligation.
For example, a transparent reformer may discover that releasing complete
records endangers vulnerable people; the quest then asks what responsible
truth-telling requires.

Use introduction packets that answer:

- Why is this person here today?
- What are they already doing competently?
- What disagreement, need, or shared problem creates contact?
- Why can the protagonist engage or walk away?
- What happens to the person if the player does nothing?
- Under what later circumstance could they plausibly reappear?

Do not announce a companion candidate as a collectible. Do not make a generic
venue a key for every person who could appear there. Admit a packet by state,
then mark the public core eligible only after the person is actually introduced.

## Relationship ecology

Treat romance as one possible relationship trajectory, not the organizing
purpose of every attractive character. Record separately:

- initial compatibility and sources of friction;
- what earns trust and what damages it;
- whether attraction is possible, impossible, unknown, or conditional;
- age, authority, mentorship, dependency, and consent boundaries;
- obligations that may outrank the relationship;
- a reason friendship or rivalry could remain satisfying without romance;
- how separation, refusal, or changed feelings remain narratively valid.

For a forgiving ensemble, avoid irreversible departure from one ordinary
mistake, but preserve accumulated consequences and hard lines. Forgiveness is
not compliance: a person may return with changed terms, distance, or a demand
for repair.

## Places, objects, and action affordances

Every recurring place needs a current function and actions it supports more
than a paragraph of visual adjectives. Record:

- who uses or controls it;
- what ordinary activity occurs there;
- what can be seen, heard, touched, changed, hidden, requested, or refused;
- what resources and constraints matter;
- which relationships or pressures become sharper there;
- how the place changes after consequential play.

Important objects similarly need an observed identity, known uses, owner or
social meaning, mutable condition, and hidden properties only when gated.

Quiet places are part of pacing. A location need not contain a clue, threat, or
future companion merely because the player inspects it.

## Mysteries and secret visibility

Design mysteries as layers of evidence and interpretation rather than one
withheld answer. Separate:

- public situation;
- character-relative beliefs;
- observable clues;
- misleading but honest appearances;
- model-visible private motivation;
- prompt-absent truth;
- reveal conditions and post-reveal consequences.

The model may receive a private motive when it needs that motive to portray
behavior, provided the disclosure boundary is explicit. A truth whose mere
presence would spoil inference stays absent until a real state gate admits it.
Embeddings, summaries, instructions, and recent keyword coincidence are not
secure gates.

Each mystery should still matter after its answer is known. The reveal may
change alliances, obligations, interpretation, or available action; it should
not merely end the only interesting question.

## Opening design

The first message should do four jobs efficiently:

1. locate the player in a concrete activity and physical situation;
2. demonstrate one or two established relationships through action or voice;
3. express the setting's central pressure without explaining the whole world;
4. stop where the player can notice, ask, speak, attempt, refuse, or wait.

Prefer an ongoing task, negotiation, lesson, repair, arrival, hearing, search,
meal, watch, or recovery to a static lore recital. Do not narrate the player's
private reaction. Do not end with a numbered choice menu unless the user asked
for one. Avoid a cliffhanger manufactured solely to force engagement.

Alternate greetings should represent materially different approved starts,
not cosmetic rewrites.

Examples are behavioral tools, not bonus fiction. Use them only to teach hard
distinctions such as observation without progression, immediate consequence,
NPC independence, or direct recall. Campaign-specific examples can leak props,
phrasing, and false facts into unrelated scenes, especially with local models.
Prefer compact symbolic examples whose people and objects do not belong to the
setting, and explicitly declare them non-canon and non-reusable.

## Quality audit

A setting is ready for artifact work when a reviewer can answer yes:

- Can play begin immediately without a lore lecture?
- Can the narrator create pressure without asking the player to author plot?
- Can the player decline the obvious invitation and still have a story?
- Does each route offer real value, cost, disagreement, and revision?
- Can important NPCs act, refuse, leave, and return for their own reasons?
- Are future characters absent rather than merely told to stay hidden?
- Do relationship possibilities remain reciprocal and non-guaranteed?
- Can the setting support quiet scenes and ordinary competence?
- Are mysteries represented as evidence, beliefs, gates, and consequences?
- Does the opening hand control back to the player cleanly?
- Could the setting survive presentation, music, image, or retrieval failure?
- Is there one accepted source rather than several drifting versions?

If the answer is no, fix the smallest owning layer instead of adding more lore.
